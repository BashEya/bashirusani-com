ABOUT

A microbiologist connecting health, environment and microbial function.

My scientific foundation is in medical microbiology, where I developed interests in pathogenic microorganisms, antimicrobial susceptibility, medical mycology and infectious disease.

My current research extends that foundation into environmental microbiology and microbial ecology, asking how microorganisms interact in contaminated environments and how those interactions can be harnessed for sustainable remediation.

As a university lecturer and peer reviewer, I also contribute through teaching, mentoring, research synthesis and scientific communication.

CURRENT RESEARCH PROJECT

Amoebae, symbionts and hydrocarbon remediation: exploring antimicrobial resistance dynamics using omics.

My PhD research investigates how free-living amoebae and their associated bacteria and fungi influence microbial community structure, hydrocarbon degradation and antimicrobial resistance in contaminated environments.

QuestionHow do overlooked amoeba–microbe interactions shape remediation and resistance ecology?
ApproachCulture-based microbiology, microscopy, qPCR, 16S/ITS/18S sequencing, hydrocarbon analysis and bioinformatics.
ImpactUnderstanding microbial interactions that may improve environmental remediation while informing One Health-relevant AMR ecology.

Doctoral project: Unveiling the Roles of Amoebae and Their Symbionts in Hydrocarbon Remediation and Antimicrobial Resistance Dynamics: An Omics Approach.
