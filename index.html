ABOUT

One scientific story, evolving across systems.

My scientific foundation is in medical microbiology, where I developed interests in pathogenic microorganisms, antimicrobial susceptibility, medical mycology and infectious disease.

My current work extends that foundation into environmental microbiology and microbial ecology, asking how microorganisms interact in contaminated environments and how those interactions can be harnessed for sustainable remediation.

As a university lecturer and peer reviewer, I also work across teaching, student mentoring, scientific writing, research synthesis and communication. Rather than treating medical and environmental microbiology as separate identities, I connect them through microbial adaptation, environmental reservoirs, antimicrobial resistance and the wider One Health framework.

CURRENT PHD PROJECT

University of Strathclyde · Civil & Environmental Engineering

Unveiling the Roles of Amoebae and Their Symbionts in Hydrocarbon Remediation and Antimicrobial Resistance Dynamics

An omics-informed investigation of microbial interactions in hydrocarbon-impacted environments.

Free-living amoebaeHydrocarbonsSymbiontsAMROmics

What I am asking

How do amoebae and their associated bacteria and fungi influence microbial community structure, hydrocarbon degradation and antimicrobial resistance dynamics in contaminated environments?

How I am approaching it

Environmental sampling, culture-based microbiology, DNA extraction, qPCR, microscopy, 16S/ITS/18S sequencing, hydrocarbon analysis and bioinformatics-informed interpretation.

Why it matters

The project aims to improve our understanding of overlooked microbial interactions that may shape both environmental remediation performance and One Health-relevant resistance ecology.

03Evaluate hydrocarbon degradation
04Link genes, pathways & ecological function
